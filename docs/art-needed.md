# Art needed

Regenerated by `npm run docs` from what is in `public/art`. **4 to go, 108 in.** Send finished files in any zip, named anything; they get resized and filed under the ids below. Full framing rules in `docs/art-spec.md`.

Order of priority: cards in the most preset decks first, and among those the ones in the first three slots of a deck row (visible on the landing page without scrolling). Cards outside every preset deck come last: they show only in the Compendium and in random decks.

## Characters

Square, 1024 px or larger. Head and shoulders, face in the upper-middle third, eyes about 40% down; the avatar is a circle crop of the centre and the smallest tile is about 40 px. Plain or softly lit background, no text, no frame. Painterly, matching the portraits already in.

## Locations

Square, 1536 px (1024 minimum). Wide establishing shot with the subject inside the central 50% both ways: desktop crops to about 3:2, phones to a tall portrait. No people in the foreground, no legible signage, mid-tone (the panel darkens it), low fine detail because a rule box and tiles sit on top.

## Threats

Square, 1024 px. One clear silhouette, high contrast, one signature colour per Threat so the 18 px chip is recognisable. A condition, not a portrait of a person. Menace without gore. A second wide 1536×768 scene of the same Threat, same palette, backs the confrontation.

| `land_office` | The Land Office | Systemic Pressure | The Homestead Act of 1862 offered 160 acres for five years of residence, to citizens, which Black Americans were not until 1866. The Southern Homestead Act then opened poor land for ten years through slow and hostile land offices; about four thousand Black families got through. On the Plains some 3,500 more proved up, at Nicodemus, DeWitty, Dearfield and Blackdom, out of 1.6 million homesteads in all. |
