# Black History Card Battler — Systems Prototype (v0.2)

A fast, replayable 1v1 card battler about Influence on history. Human vs Harborlight (AI), responsive web, no backend.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173  (add ?dev=1 for developer tools)
npm test           # engine + AI tests (vitest)
npm run sim -- 200 # headless Harborlight-vs-Harborlight balance run
npm run build      # production build to dist/
```

## Layout of the code

```
src/engine/      Pure TypeScript game engine. No React. Deterministic, seeded.
  types.ts         GameState, cards, plans, events
  content/         Data-driven Characters, Locations, Threats, Events, preset decks
  setup.ts         createMatch(seed), turn start, threat spawning
  query.ts         Influence, Force, capacity, legal-action API, plan validation
  resolve.ts       resolveTurn(state, {A, B}) → next state + ordered events; pending Stand on Business raises land here
  view.ts          viewFor(state, player): the redacted view every player (human UI and AI) receives
src/ai/          Harborlight. Plans from the redacted view only; simulates candidates with the real engine.
src/ui/          React presentation. Renders views, collects a TurnPlan, animates the event feed.
src/analytics/   Local playtest metrics (localStorage JSON, export from the dev panel)
tests/           Engine, redaction, abilities, threats, stakes, AI smoke tests
scripts/         sim.ts (batch balance), trace.ts (one match, full log)
```

The engine never imports React. The UI never touches the true `GameState` except in the controller that owns it; every screen renders `viewFor(state, player)`. Harborlight consumes exactly that same view. PvP later means replacing the AI plan provider with a remote player's submitted `TurnPlan`.

## Playing

- **Plan**: drag a card onto a Location, drag a Ready Character from your Gates into the Location, drag an Established Character to another Location, drag any Character onto a Threat. Tapping opens the same actions in a sheet. Then **Lock It In**. Two minutes per turn; timeout locks whatever you had.
- **Tutorial**: the start screen's *Tutorial* button plays seed 7 with Railroad vs Black Star, stops the clock, and walks the first five turns one lesson at a time: reading lessons modal the board out behind a card, doing lessons spotlight the exact card, tile, Threat or button and wait for it (Energy, Gates, entering, Threats, Stand on Business). After Turn 5 the regular coach takes over; the clock stays stopped for the rest of the match. Script: `src/ui/tutorial.ts`.
- **Watch**: after both players lock in, the turn plays out one beat at a time (Hearthstone/Snap style): each play, Event, Reveal, entry, move, clash and Showdown gets its own banner and spotlight, and the Location tally lands last. Events sit face-up in the Event slot they were played into (the opponent's face-down) until their beat, then flip and flare. Skip ▸▸ jumps to the settled board. `resolveTurn(state, plans, { trace: true })` returns the same beats as `TraceStep`s.
- **Power animations on the board** (one at a time, tuned by hand): Robert Duncanson's landscape sends a particle trail with a ring burst and a +1 stamp to every Location he paints (`Trails.tsx`); Zora Neale Hurston's Folklore presents the top two cards in the centre of the screen, the dearer one lights up and rides a trail into the hand, the other flips and returns to the deck (`DigReveal.tsx`; the opponent sees the backs and still sees one kept). Both key off event `data` (`trail`, `dig`) and hold the replay beat until they land. Dev hooks `window.__sobTrail` and `window.__sobDig` preview them from the console with a freeze option.
- **Win**: lead Influence at two of the three Locations after Turn 8.
- **Legacy** is what the match is worth. Stand on Business once per player to double it (1 → 2 → 4). It also adds an 8th turn, and whoever stood can no longer Sit Down. The raise lands one turn later, Marvel Snap style: the other side gets a full turn to Sit Down at the old price, keep playing, or Stand back.

Developer tools (`?dev=1` or the button on the start screen): fixed seeds, AI reasoning log (every candidate plan, score, chosen plan and tier), analytics summary/export, full event log, the true state, generic placeholder names (Test A), and a local two-player pass-the-device mode (Phase 2).

## Decisions made where the brief left room

- **Energy**: every card has a cost (0 to 8). Each turn a player gets Energy equal to the turn number (`ENERGY_CURVE` = 1 to 8; a slower curve was tried in build 132 and reverted, since a card battler player has to be able to count on next turn's Energy: pace is balanced through card costs instead), plus +1 per Established Organizer or Denmark Vesey, plus +1 the turn after holding two Inside at Oak Bluffs; unspent Energy is lost. Cheap cards early, the big names later. Gate capacity (three per Location) is the other limit on how much you can drop at once.
- **The cost curve (build 136).** With Energy pinned to the turn number, pace is set by what the cards cost, so every Character was recosted onto a real curve in one judged pass (cost = the body plus the abilities, against an 8-Influence baseline at cost 8) and the pool was widened in the middle, where it was thin: the deck-legal pool now runs 1 / 11 / 15 / 16 / 15 / 9 / 5 / 3 / 1 across costs 0 to 8 (after the four Found-out cards of build 140) (before this pass there were no four-cost cards at all). Ten heroes came in for that middle: Mary Ann Shadd Cary (4; the Provincial Freeman draws two, and everyone you relocate into her Location arrives Ready), Elizabeth Freeman (4; Brom and Bett: two friends gain lasting Influence, and nobody blocks yours there), Cathay Williams (4; fights every Threat at her Location and gives your fighters there +1 Force), James Lafayette (4; the Anansi trick on their best Ready Gate Character, and a draw whenever they play into his Location), Mary Ellen Pleasant (5; +3 Energy next turn, and a friend-of-John-Brown Force bonus against Threats), Mary Seacole (5; tends both sides: blocks and suppressions end, every Gate Character readies, and her hotel readies your arrivals), Samuel Ajayi Crowther (5; brings an Established friend across, and Threats cannot touch yours at Christ Church), Yaa Asantewaa (6; the siege: every opposing Established Character there loses 1 Influence for good, and nobody relocates out against her), Zumbi dos Palmares (6; the quilombo lifts your 0–2 cost Characters there for good, and Palmares holds against displacement) and Menelik II (7; Adwa: challenges everyone Inside at once, the weaker sent back to the Gates Fresh, and +2 to your others Inside). Four white **Allies** joined at the same time, tagged Ally and immune to Sundown Town, their histories naming their limits: Abraham Lincoln (7; where you trail, your Fresh Gate Characters ready; the Union holds against displacement), William Lloyd Garrison (3), Thaddeus Stevens (4; every Threat at his Location needs one less Force, for good; +1 Energy) and Charles Sumner (5; up to three friends gain lasting Influence; +1 to your Gate Characters). Three effect types were added for them (`relocatedInReady`, `siegeInside`, `challengeAllInside`); everything else reuses existing ones, so the Toussaint and Yemoja beats now name whichever card acted. The presets were recomposed around the new middle (each deck holds at least three 4-costs and three 5-pluses; Yemoja left Pantheon for Crowther, Toussaint left Bois Caïman for Zumbi, Lincoln readies Boukman's Gates in Bois Caïman), and every deck-legal card sits in at least one preset except the informants Pharoah and Tom and Ben Woolfolk, who wait for the deck builder.
- **Discounts**: costs are computed live (`cardCost(id, state, player)`), so a card can get cheaper while it sits in your hand. Established discounters (Booker T. Washington: all Characters −1; Cécile Fatiman: Rebellion −1; Omar ibn Said: Events −1), earned per-card discounts stored on the player (`discounts`, from Fatiman's Reveal, Carver's end-of-turn Rotation, Diallo's Letter home and Nehanda's return), and Boukman Dutty's own passive (−1 per Rebellion Character on your board) all stack, never below 0. Boukman's printed 7 is the payoff at the end of that flow: his Uprising sends every Gate Character you have Inside at once, Ready or Fresh; only blocks and full Insides stop one.

- **Inside counts more than the Gates**: every Established Character contributes `INSIDE_INFLUENCE_BONUS` (+1) Influence over its card, so entering is the point and the Gates are the staging area.
- **Three Gate slots** per player at each Location (`GATE_CAPACITY`; two until build 128) plus the Event slot. Self-play with two slots left 8% of late player-turns with no legal play; three brings that to 1%, adds a quarter more plays across Turns 5 to 8 and half again as many final-turn flips.
- **The Last Word**: the ninth turn exists only because somebody stood on business, and it plays like it. Both sides get `LAST_WORD_ENERGY` (10) Energy, the cap lifted, and `LAST_WORD_DRAW` (1) extra card at its start. The HUD calls it THE LAST WORD. Harborlight stands less freely than it did (thresholds 0.84 / 0.94, bluff 2%, never before Turn 5), so the tenth turn is one confident call rather than automatic.
- **Eight turns** (nine after Stand on Business: 8+1, settled on after nine felt a turn long) with 24-card decks (22 until build 129; The Last Word deals an extra card, and the fourth deck slot per preset went to the decks' own themes): four in the opening hand and one draw every turn. (Seven turns and 18 cards until build 126: self-play showed the 18-card deck running dry in most nine-turn matches, so the deck grew with the clock.)
- **The Middle Passage** is framed as what survived, not only what was lost: the toll at its Gates stays, and whoever leaves arrives Ready and carries +1 Influence for good. The card copy names what came across (the stories, the orisha, Islam, the music) beside the numbers. Hands hold at most 7 cards; a draw into a full hand is discarded (logged in the turn log).

- **Every card is somebody, or something, that existed, with two deliberate exceptions.** The invented people are gone: the Newsboy is John Russwurm (Freedom's Journal, 1827), the Barber is Alonzo Herndon, the Block Captain is Callie House, the Neighbor Kid is Bud Billiken (the Chicago Defender's 1923 mascot), Karen is Roger Taney, the Pullman Porter is the Sleeping Car Porters (the 1925 Brotherhood) and the Cookout became The Stroll, now a Location (State Street, Chicago, 1910s–20s: +1 Influence Inside after dark). Brand characters and institutions count as long as they are real history. The Organizer and the OG stay by request as archetypes (no single person is meant). Chairteenth stays too: it is the Montgomery riverfront brawl of 5 August 2023, real but recent and with living participants, so nobody is named on the card. The public-domain line is otherwise people dead before about 1930; Henry Ossawa Tanner (d. 1937) and George Washington Carver (d. 1943) are the flagged exceptions. Threats and Events are conditions, not people, and keep their names.
- **Variety pass.** Duplicated powers were split up: Zora digs (top two cards, keep the dearer), Madam C.J. Walker banks +2 Energy for next turn, Daniel Payne's next Character costs 1 less, Victor Hugo Green grants an extra Relocation next turn, Denmark Vesey draws a card per other friend at his Location (max 2), Richard Allen draws whenever one of yours goes Inside at Mother Bethel, Callie House draws 2 when a Threat at her Location is cleared, and Oshun grows your weakest Character at her Location by +1 every turn. Pure "draw a card" belongs to John Russwurm (one) and Mary Ann Shadd Cary (two).
- **Artists.** A new category: Edmonia Lewis, Henry Ossawa Tanner, Robert Duncanson, Edward Bannister, Harriet Powers and David Drake (Dave the Potter). Their work outlasts them: an artist's Reveal (or Tanner's standing ability, every turn) adds *lasting Influence to the Location itself* for that player, which stays when the artist leaves or is knocked away. Lewis +2, Drake +1, Bannister +1 (+2 if you are behind there when he lands), Powers +1 per other friendly Character there (max 3), Duncanson +1 at every Location where you are Established.
- **The Middle Passage and the DeWolf Trade.** A Location with no Inside: every Character at its Gates loses 1 Influence for good each turn, Threats never appear there, and whoever leaves arrives Ready. The DeWolf Trade is a Threat (5 Force) that ships the lowest Fresh Gate Character at its Location to The Middle Passage when it is in play, otherwise to a random Location, with a Setback either way.
- **Informants and Charleston, 1822.** Persuade is gone. Four Informant Characters take its place: Peter Prioleau and George Wilson (the Vesey plot, 1822), Pharoah and Tom, and Ben Woolfolk (Gabriel's rising, 1800). An Informant is played onto the opponent's Gates at a Location with one of their slots open: it is theirs, its negative Influence counts against them there, it takes one of their Gate slots, and it never becomes Ready or goes Inside. They can relocate it (their Relocation), slide it with Robert Smalls, or let a Threat knock it away; Harriet will not conduct one, no opposing Reveal targets it (it is not the opponent's), and it never confronts. The holder's own answer arrived in build 140, **Found out**: David Ruggles and Lewis Hayden find the Informant at their Gates out and send it back to the planter's hand (their discard if that hand is full), William Parker has it arrested into the planter's discard and then moves on to another Location, and William Still, Established there, writes it down so it counts 0 while it keeps its slot. None is dead without an Informant: Ruggles draws, Hayden's house holds yours there against displacement this turn, Parker's league confronts a Threat with +1 Force, Still's interview digs two. Turning an informer was deliberately not built: none of the verified people did it, and both players are the People. Informants are placed after every regular Character, so they never win a slot race against the holder's own plays; one that finds the Gates full goes back to your hand. Charleston, 1822 is a Location where, at the end of every turn, the Fresh Gate Character with the lowest Influence changes sides if the other side has a Gate slot for it (a tie goes against the leader); an Informant there is found out and returns to its planter's hand, and a Character played there is at risk unless something lower is beside it. Sundown Town leaves Informants alone. Marie Laveau's Reveal is now a hex: the strongest opposing Gate Character at her Location loses 2 Influence for the rest of the match.
- **Sundown Town and allies.** Curfew never holds Harriet Tubman herself. John Brown does not experience the town's rules: no curfew, never run out, and while he is at that Location one friendly Character who would be run out at the end of the turn (the highest Influence first) hides with him overnight instead.
- **Gate wait timing.** A Character played on turn N is Fresh through turn N+1's planning and becomes Ready at the end of N+1, so it can enter on N+2. This is the reading that makes "becomes Ready immediately" effects (Harriet, Organizer, Juneteenth) worth a full turn. If playtesting says the wait is too long, the one-line change is in the cleanup step of `resolve.ts`.
- **Stand on Business** adds an 8th turn and removes Sit Down for the player who stood. The raise is part of the hidden plan and takes effect at the end of the *following* turn. Nobody is forced into a yes/no modal: the other player plans a normal turn with a banner showing the pending raise, and Sitting Down during that turn costs only the old Legacy. Standing back doubles again (max 4). A Stand on turn 7 adds turn 8 and lands with the final result.
- **Day and night**: odd turns are day, even turns are night (`isNight`). A curfew is a condition of a Location, not a Threat: Sundown Town (`curfew: true`) lets nobody relocate out at night. The Justice System's two-turn hold is a separate rule. `lockReason(state, c)` is the one gate for both. Only curfew Locations show the time of day: a DAY or NIGHT tag on the panel, and at night `locations/<id>_night.jpg` when it exists, otherwise the day art darkened. The rest of the board looks the same day or night.
- **Harriet Tubman** (Conductor) is the only card that ignores locks: her Reveal moves any friendly Character, Gate or Inside, straight Inside another Location when there is room, otherwise to its Gates Ready. Free and before entries.
- **Relocation** works from both zones. Inside → Gates arrives Fresh (the committed zone pays to leave). Gates → Gates keeps Ready status (the mobile zone): a Ready Character held at the Gates can be committed late wherever it is needed. One Relocation per turn, plus Porters, Lagos and Herndon exceptions.
- **Confrontation** is a planned action: commit Characters at a Threat's Location; they cannot enter or relocate that turn. Shared Threats sum both players' Force in one turn; split Threats need the owner first, then Assist is offered.
- **"History moves"**: at the start of Turn 3 one random Threat (Patrol, Complicity, Housing Restriction or the DeWolf Trade) appears at a revealed Location without one, on Turn 5 a second one arrives in about 60% of matches, and on Turn 7 a third in about 75%. Greenwood spawns a Mob on Turn 4. Harpers Ferry reveals with Paddy Rollers.
- **Reconstruction**: a Location Lost to a Threat is not gone for good. Two turns after it falls (`RECONSTRUCTION_TURNS`), at the start of the turn, the people who stayed rebuild it: it is back in play, its Threats have moved on, and every Character still there gains +1 Influence. While a Location is Lost its Threats lie dormant (the Mob has done its work), so staying costs nothing more than the two dead turns. Greenwood's Mob arrives on Turn 4 and closes the district at the end of Turn 6 if nobody answers it (three turns unanswered); the rebuilt Greenwood returns on Turn 8, as Greenwood itself did after 1921.
- **Anansi retells**: played where the opponent leads, Anansi's Reveal draws a card and retells the Location into a random one not in this match (weighted like the setup draw, from the match RNG, so both sides of a PvP match see the same story) and spins his web over it: at a **webbed** Location, Characters costing 1 or less gain +2 Influence and Characters costing 3 or more lose 1, for both players. Characters, Influence and Threats stay; a Mob still on its way to a retold Greenwood still comes. Played where you already lead, he just draws and tricks their best Ready Gate Character into waiting again. Which Location he gets is the gamble.
- **Setback**: a loss suffered at the hands of injustice, meaning a Threat or a hostile Location working against you. The opponent's cards never cause one. **Reparations** converts Setbacks into lasting Influence on a Location: it counts at the end no matter when it is played.
- **The Justice System** Location holds anyone who goes Inside for two turns: no relocating out.
- **The Tabernacle** protects your Characters from displacement. Establish the church set there (Richard Allen, Absalom Jones, Daniel Payne) and **Black Jesus** appears: sanctuary at his Location and +1 Influence to every Character you control. He is never in a deck.
- **The Ancestors** are never in a deck either: in 25% of matches (rolled at setup), holding three Characters Inside at Accra, Ghana brings the card to your hand once. Chairteenth has the same 25% roll and arrives when Montgomery, Alabama reveals. Played while planning, it shows Harborlight's plan for the turn (the AI plans deterministically, so peeking changes nothing) and every timed danger on the board.
- **Straight Inside** (Sleeping Car Porters) always goes Inside the turn it is played. **Direct Entry** (Bessie Coleman) may: the planned move carries a Gates/Inside toggle. Nobody else skips the Gates.
- **The Mob** closes a Location after three unanswered turns.
- **Energy** shows as a Hearthstone-style crystal bar under the timer: lit crystals are unspent this turn, dim ones spent, hollow ones arrive on later turns.
- `npm run docs` regenerates `docs/copy.md` (every player-facing string, for copy editing) and `docs/art-spec.md` (image specs plus the missing-art list).
- **Events** are played into the purple Event slot beside your Gates at a Location: one per Location per player per turn, and the Gates can be full. Each works everywhere and gets a bonus from where it lands: Reparations +1 in the Americas, The Ancestors +1 Influence in Africa, Word of Mouth draws 2 with a crowd, Community Defense +2 Force at its Location. Decks carry at most two.
- **Gatherings** are Characters that are never in a deck. **Chairteenth** (1/3, +1 Force to friendly confronters) arrives Ready at both players' Gates the moment Juneteenth is revealed. Each arrival opens a fanfare card. If a card ever returns a Gathering to hand, it can be replayed like any Character.
- **Location scoring with two ties or a Lost Location**: whoever won more Locations wins; if equal, the brief's tiebreaks apply.
- **Katherine Johnson's** private reveal is stored per player and redacted from the opponent's view; the AI is allowed to use it exactly as a human would.
- **Victor Hugo Green** (the Green Book) lets relocations out of his Location arrive Inside, the interior-to-interior answer to Sundown Town. **Sojourner Truth** replaces the "selected additional historical figures" slot to give the pool a Suppress effect. **Ida B. Wells, Queen Nzinga, Toussaint Louverture, Marcus Garvey, Bessie Coleman** round out the sixteen.
- **Sundown Town** is drawn about a third as often as other Locations and does nothing on the turn it reveals. **Gary, Indiana** (Steel and Soul) joins the pool. **Paddy Roller** is one shared Threat "in the area" rather than one per player.
- Player Gates are shown under each Location panel (the wireframe omitted them; the brief requires them).

## Design intents to keep

Things that play well and should survive any rebalancing:

- **Greenwood is hard on purpose.** The Mob arrives on Turn 4, closes the district after three unanswered turns, and needs 6 Force in one turn, more than either side usually has alone, so both players have to fight it together or lose the Location. When Greenwood is the last Location to reveal, players who know it start stocking up in anticipation, which is exactly the feeling we want. Do not soften the Mob, its timing, or the 6. Reconstruction does not soften it either: the district is gone for two full turns and comes back only for the last one, and Anansi retelling Greenwood does not cancel the Mob.

## PvP and the Unity port

The rules live in `src/engine` and nothing else, and that is the part that carries over. What the engine already guarantees, and what every change has to keep:

- **Pure and deterministic.** `resolveTurn(state, plans)` is a function of its inputs. All randomness comes from the match RNG seeded at `createMatch` (Location draw, Threat waves, Anansi's retell, displacement targets); the AI seeds its own RNG from the seed and turn. There is no `Math.random`, no wall clock, in the engine. Two clients with the same seed and the same two plans compute the same next state, so PvP is "exchange plans, resolve locally, compare a hash", or resolve once on a server and ship the events.
- **State is plain data.** `GameState` is JSON: no classes, no functions, no references. It serializes, diffs and replays. `viewFor(state, player)` is the redaction a server would apply before sending a client its view (hand hidden, unrevealed Locations hidden, deck order hidden); the AI is built on that same view, so nothing it does depends on secrets.
- **Plans are data.** A `TurnPlan` is a small JSON object (plays, enters, relocations, confronts, stand, sit). Validation (`validatePlan`) runs on both sides.
- **Events are the animation script.** `resolveTurn` returns an ordered event list and, with `trace: true`, one `TraceStep` per beat with the state after it. The UI replays those beats; the board animations (clash strikes, power trails) key off event `data` (`clash`, `trail`), never off text. A Unity front end reads the same events.
- **Content is tables.** Characters, Locations, Threats, Events and decks are typed data in `src/engine/content`; every effect is a tagged `{ type, ... }` object handled by a switch in `resolve.ts` / `query.ts`. That maps one-to-one onto ScriptableObjects and an effect enum in C#.

Porting order when the time comes: engine (TypeScript to C#, mechanical; the tests in `tests/engine.test.ts` are the spec, port them first), content tables, then the UI on top of the event stream. Keep `scripts/sim.ts` alive in both worlds: balance is measured, not argued.

## Audio

Music and sound live in `src/ui/audio` and never touch the engine. Two switches (music, effects) sit under the tagline on the landing page and beside the legacy coin in the match, remembered in `localStorage`. The music is one looping track for now, *Echoes of the Past* (`public/audio/echoes-of-the-past.m4a`; the single-file build inlines it on `window.__AUDIO__` like the art), faded in on the landing page and the match and out on the reading screens; it starts on the first tap or key, which is when browsers allow sound.

Sound effects are asked for by name (`sfx('card.drop')`), and the names are the contract: `SFX_EVENTS` in `src/ui/audio/sfx.ts` lists every cue and what it is for (tap, card pick and drop, lock, turn, enter, move, clash hit and banish, threat spawn and clear, trail, dig, stand, the Last Word, a Location lost, win, lose, draw). Buttons map themselves with `data-sfx="name"` (or `off`); any other button plays `tap`; the match plays one cue per replay beat and its own cues on strikes, trails and digs. The cues are recorded clips from Imphenzia's Universal Sound FX (licensed; 33 trimmed, levelled MP3s in `public/audio/sfx`, about a megabyte, chosen for a palette between Civilization, Hearthstone and Marvel Snap: real card deals and a shuffle for the hand, a case latch for Lock In, a metal gate for walking Inside, air whooshes for relocations, wood and stone impacts for strikes, church bells for The Last Word, brass and piano stingers for the verdicts). `SFX_FILES` maps each name to its layers and round-robin variants; a Web Audio synth plays the same cue until the clips decode or for any name without one, so nothing is ever silent. A Unity port keeps the same names as an `AudioEvent` enum mapped to `AudioClip`s (FMOD or Wwise events if middleware comes in).

Other libraries worth knowing, all royalty-free for games and usable in both the web build and Unity: **Pro Sound Collection** by Gamemaster Audio (about 8,000 sounds, with casino and card categories), and for free starts **Kenney's** CC0 packs and the yearly **Sonniss GDC Game Audio Bundle**.

## Harborlight

Harborlight receives `viewFor(state, 'B')`: no opponent hand, no unrevealed Location identities, no draw order, no RNG. It decides confrontations heuristically, then enumerates plays × entries × relocations, simulates each with `resolveTurn` (opponent passing) and scores the resulting board (win probability from per-Location Influence gaps that steepen toward the final turn, projected Established value, Threat exposure, Gate crowding). It picks the best plan ~70% of the time, a sensible alternative ~20%, and an imperfect legal plan ~10%. Stand on Business triggers above ~65% / ~80% estimated win chance with a ~7% bluff rate near even. Tuning constants are in `DEFAULT_TUNING`.

On the 8+1 clock with Energy equal to the turn number and the build 136 presets (30 AI matches per pairing): Railroad vs Black Star 19–11, Black Star vs Bois Caïman 18–12, Bois Caïman vs Pantheon 19–11, Railroad vs Pantheon 18–12, Railroad vs Mirror 13–17, no draws; a Mirror-vs-Mirror run of 40 puts every card's win rate when played between 41% and 60%, and win rate by cost across the pairings runs flat from 48% (cost 1) to 57% (cost 5), so no cost bracket is a trap. Pantheon is still the deck to tune. Plays per side run 1.3 to 1.5 a turn, hands hold near five cards, lead changes 5 to 6 a match, final-turn flips 0.6 to 1.0. (The first cut of these presets had Black Star at 7–23 against Railroad: it had traded three cheap artists for expensive cards, and tempo is bodies, so the artists came back and Williams and Seacole moved to Mirror and Pantheon.) Harborlight's stand thresholds are 0.84 / 0.94 (bluff 2%, never before Turn 5); in PvP the stand is a human call.

## Mythic and the Summon

Characters carry a category: historical, archetype or mythic. Mythic cards (Anansi, Shango, Oshun, Yemoja, Ogun, Mami Wata, Black Jesus) are fantasy drawn from African cosmology and diaspora spirituality, labelled as such on the card. The joint **Summon** is cooperative: both players commit at a Location with a Threat via quick chat; enough combined Force manifests Obatala (Threats cleared, the Location can never be Lost, +1 Influence to everyone there, both draw). A failed Summon followed by that Location being Lost costs both players 1 Influence at their other Locations.
