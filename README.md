# Black History Card Battler — Systems Prototype (v0.2)

A fast, replayable 1v1 card battler about Influence on history. Human vs Harborlight (AI), responsive web, no backend.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173  (add ?dev=1 for developer tools)
npm test           # engine + AI tests (vitest)
npm run sim -- 200 # headless Harborlight-vs-Harborlight balance run
npm run build      # production build to dist/
```

## Layout of the code

```
src/engine/      Pure TypeScript game engine. No React. Deterministic, seeded.
  types.ts         GameState, cards, plans, events
  content/         Data-driven Characters, Locations, Threats, Events, preset decks
  setup.ts         createMatch(seed), turn start, threat spawning
  query.ts         Influence, Force, capacity, legal-action API, plan validation
  resolve.ts       resolveTurn(state, {A, B}) → next state + ordered events; pending Stand on Business raises land here
  view.ts          viewFor(state, player): the redacted view every player (human UI and AI) receives
src/ai/          Harborlight. Plans from the redacted view only; simulates candidates with the real engine.
src/ui/          React presentation. Renders views, collects a TurnPlan, animates the event feed.
src/analytics/   Local playtest metrics (localStorage JSON, export from the dev panel)
tests/           Engine, redaction, abilities, threats, stakes, AI smoke tests
scripts/         sim.ts (batch balance), trace.ts (one match, full log)
```

The engine never imports React. The UI never touches the true `GameState` except in the controller that owns it; every screen renders `viewFor(state, player)`. Harborlight consumes exactly that same view. PvP later means replacing the AI plan provider with a remote player's submitted `TurnPlan`.

## Playing

- **Plan**: drag a card onto a Location, drag a Ready Character from your Gates into the Location, drag an Established Character to another Location, drag any Character onto a Threat. Tapping opens the same actions in a sheet. Then **Lock It In**. Two minutes per turn; timeout locks whatever you had.
- **The hand** is one machine for mouse, touch and the Unity port, drawn from how Hearthstone, Runeterra, Snap and Slay the Spire do it: a card is *fanned*, *hovered* (mouse only, no memory: it straightens, lifts and grows to 1.32x the moment the pointer arrives and eases back the moment it leaves; the wrapper never moves, so the hit box is stable), *open* (a tap expands it into the Codex card, the 3D card with its history, and while it is open the card stands with its Locations lit and the tray offers "Play at …" and "Put back"; closing the Codex puts it down, so nothing is ever left standing; a card that cannot be played opens with the reason), *held* (the fan card dims while its ghost rides the pointer; a refused drop or Escape flies the ghost back to its slot with a low thud), or *reading* (the same Codex card, also from right-click or a half-second touch hold). Keys 1-3 play the standing card at that Location; Enter stands a focused card or plays into a lit Location. An unplayable card that is tapped shakes and the toast says why.
- **Tutorial**: the start screen's *Tutorial* button plays seed 7 with Railroad vs Black Star, stops the clock, and walks the first five turns one lesson at a time: reading lessons modal the board out behind a card, doing lessons spotlight the exact card, tile, Threat or button and wait for it (Energy, Gates, entering, Threats, Stand on Business). After Turn 5 the regular coach takes over; the clock stays stopped for the rest of the match. Script: `src/ui/tutorial.ts`.
- **Watch**: after both players lock in, the turn plays out one beat at a time (Hearthstone/Snap style): each play, Event, Reveal, entry, move, clash and Showdown gets its own banner and spotlight, and the Location tally lands last. Events sit face-up in the Event slot they were played into (the opponent's face-down) until their beat, then flip and flare. Skip ▸▸ jumps to the settled board. `resolveTurn(state, plans, { trace: true })` returns the same beats as `TraceStep`s.
- **The card face is the designer's frame.** `CardFace` lays every card out on the designer's WebP frames (`public/art/frames`, the art window knocked out so the portrait sits under it), with every slot placed in percentages of the card's width, so the hand card and the Codex card are one layout at two sizes. The frames carry no nameplate: the name is set big and black at the top of the parchment, the tag and era under it in gold, then the rules (the summary at hand size; every ability and the blurb in the Codex, scrolling when they run long). Cost, Influence and Force sit in the frame's green, gold and red medallions; a kind icon sits in the ribbon under the cost. Events take their own purple frame (`frames/event.webp`, the designer's 1086 × 1448 export fitted into the same 1103 × 1426 box so one layout serves both). Measurements and the reasoning: `docs/card-template.md`.
- **Power animations on the board** (one at a time, tuned by hand): Robert Duncanson's landscape sends a particle trail to every Location he paints, landing as embers rising off the Location with a +1 (`Trails.tsx`, whose `spray` look is the sparks and shockwave of a blow and whose `wave` is a healed Location's light spreading, so a power reaching across, a blow landing and good news never look alike; a cleared Threat gets its own fireworks; the full set is under "Effects library" below); Zora Neale Hurston's Folklore presents the top two cards in the centre of the screen, the dearer one lights up and rides a trail into the hand, the other flips and returns to the deck (`DigReveal.tsx`; the opponent sees the backs and still sees one kept). Both key off event `data` (`trail`, `dig`) and hold the replay beat until they land. Dev hooks `window.__sobTrail` and `window.__sobDig` preview them from the console with a freeze option.
- **Win**: lead Influence at two of the three Locations after Turn 8.
- **Legacy** is what the match is worth. Stand on Business once per player to multiply it, and the earlier the bolder: ×4 on turns 1–2, ×3 on turns 3–5, ×2 from turn 6 (`STAND_MULTIPLIERS`, capped at `MAX_STAKES` 16), win or lose. It also adds a 9th turn, and whoever stood can no longer Sit Down. The raise lands one turn later, Marvel Snap style: the other side gets a full turn to Sit Down at the old price, keep playing, or Stand back. Sit Down is a retreat: the match ends the moment you confirm it (`retreat()` in the engine, no plan, no deliberation) and the other side takes the current Legacy.

Developer tools (`?dev=1` or the button on the start screen): fixed seeds, AI reasoning log (every candidate plan, score, chosen plan and tier), analytics summary/export, full event log, the true state, generic placeholder names (Test A), and a local two-player pass-the-device mode (Phase 2).

## Decisions made where the brief left room

- **Energy**: every card has a cost (0 to 8). Each turn a player gets Energy equal to the turn number (`ENERGY_CURVE` = 1 to 8; a slower curve was tried in build 132 and reverted, since a card battler player has to be able to count on next turn's Energy: pace is balanced through card costs instead), plus +1 per Established Organizer or Denmark Vesey, plus +1 the turn after holding two Inside at Oak Bluffs; unspent Energy is lost. Cheap cards early, the big names later. Gate capacity (three per Location) is the other limit on how much you can drop at once.
- **The cost curve (build 136).** With Energy pinned to the turn number, pace is set by what the cards cost, so every Character was recosted onto a real curve in one judged pass (cost = the body plus the abilities, against an 8-Influence baseline at cost 8) and the pool was widened in the middle, where it was thin: the deck-legal pool now runs 1 / 7 / 11 / 16 / 19 / 11 / 8 / 3 / 1 across costs 0 to 8 (after a second pass that moved 24 cards up a step so the cheap end thinned out: the 1s are Bud Billiken, the Organizer, Payne, Taney, Russwurm, Dunbar and the two 1-cost Informants) (before this pass there were no four-cost cards at all). Ten heroes came in for that middle: Mary Ann Shadd Cary (4; the Provincial Freeman draws two, and everyone you relocate into her Location arrives Ready), Elizabeth Freeman (4; Brom and Bett: two friends gain lasting Influence, and nobody blocks yours there), Cathay Williams (4; fights every Threat at her Location and gives your fighters there +1 Force), James Lafayette (4; the Anansi trick on their best Ready Gate Character, and a draw whenever they play into his Location), Mary Ellen Pleasant (5; +3 Energy next turn, and a friend-of-John-Brown Force bonus against Threats), Mary Seacole (5; tends both sides: blocks and suppressions end, every Gate Character readies, and her hotel readies your arrivals), Samuel Ajayi Crowther (5; brings an Established friend across, and Threats cannot touch yours at Christ Church), Yaa Asantewaa (6; the siege: every opposing Established Character there loses 1 Influence for good, and nobody relocates out against her), Zumbi dos Palmares (6; the quilombo lifts your 0–2 cost Characters there for good, and Palmares holds against displacement) and Menelik II (7; Adwa: challenges everyone Inside at once, the weaker sent back to the Gates Waiting, and +2 to your others Inside). Four white **Allies** joined at the same time, tagged Ally and immune to Sundown Town, their histories naming their limits: Abraham Lincoln (7; where you trail, your Waiting Gate Characters ready; the Union holds against displacement), William Lloyd Garrison (3), Thaddeus Stevens (4; every Threat at his Location needs one less Force, for good; +1 Energy) and Charles Sumner (5; up to three friends gain lasting Influence; +1 to your Gate Characters). Three effect types were added for them (`relocatedInReady`, `siegeInside`, `challengeAllInside`); everything else reuses existing ones, so the Toussaint and Yemoja beats now name whichever card acted. The presets were recomposed around the new middle (each deck holds at least three 4-costs and three 5-pluses; Yemoja left Pantheon for Crowther, Toussaint left Bois Caïman for Zumbi, Lincoln readies Boukman's Gates in Bois Caïman), and every deck-legal card sits in at least one preset except the informants Pharoah and Tom and Ben Woolfolk, who wait for the deck builder.
- **Discounts**: costs are computed live (`cardCost(id, state, player)`), so a card can get cheaper while it sits in your hand. Established discounters (Booker T. Washington: all Characters −1; Cécile Fatiman: Rebellion −1; Omar ibn Said: Events −1), earned per-card discounts stored on the player (`discounts`, from Fatiman's Reveal, Carver's end-of-turn Rotation, Diallo's Letter home and Nehanda's return), and Boukman Dutty's own passive (−1 per Rebellion Character on your board) all stack, never below 0. Boukman's printed 7 is the payoff at the end of that flow: his Uprising sends every Gate Character you have Inside at once, Ready or Waiting; only blocks and full Insides stop one.

- **Inside counts more than the Gates**: every Established Character contributes `INSIDE_INFLUENCE_BONUS` (+1) Influence over its card, so entering is the point and the Gates are the staging area.
- **Three Gate slots** per player at each Location (`GATE_CAPACITY`; two until build 128) plus the Event slot. Self-play with two slots left 8% of late player-turns with no legal play; three brings that to 1%, adds a quarter more plays across Turns 5 to 8 and half again as many final-turn flips.
- **The Last Word**: the ninth turn exists only because somebody stood on business, and it plays like it. Both sides get `LAST_WORD_ENERGY` (10) Energy, the cap lifted, and `LAST_WORD_DRAW` (1) extra card at its start. The HUD calls it THE LAST WORD. Harborlight stands less freely than it did (thresholds 0.84 / 0.94, bluff 2%, never before Turn 5), so the tenth turn is one confident call rather than automatic.
- **Eight turns** (nine after Stand on Business: 8+1, settled on after nine felt a turn long) with 24-card decks (22 until build 129; The Last Word deals an extra card, and the fourth deck slot per preset went to the decks' own themes): four in the opening hand and one draw every turn. (Seven turns and 18 cards until build 126: self-play showed the 18-card deck running dry in most nine-turn matches, so the deck grew with the clock.)
- **The Middle Passage** is framed as what survived, not only what was lost: the toll at its Gates stays, and whoever leaves arrives Ready and carries +1 Influence for good. The card copy names what came across (the stories, the orisha, Islam, the music) beside the numbers. Hands hold at most 7 cards; a draw into a full hand is discarded (logged in the turn log).

- **Every card is somebody, or something, that existed, with two deliberate exceptions.** The invented people are gone: the Newsboy is John Russwurm (Freedom's Journal, 1827), the Barber is Alonzo Herndon, the Block Captain is Callie House, the Neighbor Kid is Bud Billiken (the Chicago Defender's 1923 mascot), Karen is Roger Taney, the Pullman Porter is the Sleeping Car Porters (the 1925 Brotherhood) and the Cookout became The Stroll, now a Location (State Street, Chicago, 1910s–20s: +1 Influence Inside after dark). Brand characters and institutions count as long as they are real history. The Organizer and the OG stay by request as archetypes (no single person is meant). Chairteenth stays too: it is the Montgomery riverfront brawl of 5 August 2023, real but recent and with living participants, so nobody is named on the card. The public-domain line is otherwise people dead before about 1930; Henry Ossawa Tanner (d. 1937) and George Washington Carver (d. 1943) are the flagged exceptions. The Cotton Club Orchestra is the house band as an institution (Ellington's, then Calloway's), the bandleaders named in the history text only, the way the Sleeping Car Porters are the Brotherhood. Threats and Events are conditions, not people, and keep their names.
- **Variety pass.** Duplicated powers were split up: Zora digs (top two cards, keep the dearer), Madam C.J. Walker banks +2 Energy for next turn, Daniel Payne's next Character costs 1 less, Victor Hugo Green grants an extra Relocation next turn, Denmark Vesey draws a card per other friend at his Location (max 2), Richard Allen draws whenever one of yours goes Inside at Mother Bethel, Callie House draws 2 when a Threat at her Location is cleared, and Oshun grows your weakest Character at her Location by +1 every turn. Pure "draw a card" belongs to John Russwurm (one) and Mary Ann Shadd Cary (two).
- **Artists.** A new category: Edmonia Lewis, Henry Ossawa Tanner, Robert Duncanson, Edward Bannister, Harriet Powers and David Drake (Dave the Potter). Their work outlasts them: an artist's Reveal (or Tanner's standing ability, every turn) adds *lasting Influence to the Location itself* for that player, which stays when the artist leaves or is knocked away. Lewis +2, Drake +1, Bannister +1 (+2 if you are behind there when he lands), Powers +1 per other friendly Character there (max 3), Duncanson +1 at every Location where you are Established.
- **The Middle Passage and the DeWolf Trade.** A Location with no Inside: every Character at its Gates loses 1 Influence for good each turn, Threats never appear there, and whoever leaves arrives Ready. The DeWolf Trade is a Threat (5 Force) that ships the lowest Waiting Gate Character at its Location to The Middle Passage when it is in play, otherwise to a random Location, with a Setback either way.
- **Bois Caïman, the Event.** Cost 3, needs a Location with a Threat: until that Threat is neutralized, every Character there, both sides, confronts it every turn with full Force and nobody relocates out (Harriet still conducts). The panel shows OATH. The preset deck that carried the name is now **Uprising**, so the word means the night, the pair and the card.
- **Team-ups.** Thirteen pairs that belong together (`src/engine/content/teamups.ts`). Both Established at the same Location for the same side and the team-up fires. Standing ones (Bois Caïman's sworn oath, The Raid, Tuskegee, The Pilots, Sweet Auburn, The Maroons) hold while both stay Inside; once-only ones (Adwa, Free African Society, Vigilance, Reconstruction, Iron and Thunder, Rent Party, The Press) fire for whoever assembles them first and then close for the match, whichever side holds the cards. The panel shows the name while a standing one holds; the Codex says who each card teams with; the Compendium has a chapter.
- **Informants and Charleston, 1822.** Persuade is gone. Four Informant Characters take its place: Peter Prioleau and George Wilson (the Vesey plot, 1822), Pharoah and Tom, and Ben Woolfolk (Gabriel's rising, 1800). An Informant is played onto the opponent's Gates at a Location with one of their slots open: it is theirs, its negative Influence counts against them there, it takes one of their Gate slots, and it never becomes Ready or goes Inside. They can relocate it (their Relocation), slide it with Robert Smalls, or let a Threat knock it away; Harriet will not conduct one, no opposing Reveal targets it (it is not the opponent's), and it never confronts. The holder's own answer arrived in build 140, **Found out**: David Ruggles and Lewis Hayden find the Informant at their Gates out and send it back to the planter's hand (their discard if that hand is full), William Parker has it arrested into the planter's discard and then moves on to another Location, and William Still, Established there, writes it down so it counts 0 while it keeps its slot. None is dead without an Informant: Ruggles draws, Hayden's house holds yours there against displacement this turn, Parker's league confronts a Threat with +1 Force, Still's interview digs two. Turning an informer was deliberately not built: none of the verified people did it, and both players are the People. Amnesty is different and is verified: Desmond Tutu's Reveal hears the Informant at his Gates out in full and grants amnesty, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's own terms, and it stays where it is, Waiting, as its holder's own Character from then on (its Influence counts for them, it can go Inside); with none there, the cathedral holds like Hayden's house. The Tutu card is hidden for now (he died in 2021, past the public-domain line): out of decks, the Compendium and the art list, the mechanic kept. Claudette Colvin (born 1939, living) is hidden the same way; Empress Taytu Betul took her Black Star slot. Roger Taney is retired as a card too: nobody wants to play him, so he is a Threat instead, **The Dred Scott Decision** (stands two turns, then turns everyone at the Location out to open Gates elsewhere and lifts; 4 Force stops it); Callie House took his Mirror slot. Established, St George's shields the Location like Nanny's Maroons. Informants are placed after every regular Character, so they never win a slot race against the holder's own plays; one that finds the Gates full goes back to your hand. Charleston, 1822 is a Location where, at the end of every turn, the Waiting Gate Character with the lowest Influence changes sides if the other side has a Gate slot for it (a tie goes against the leader); an Informant there is found out and returns to its planter's hand, and a Character played there is at risk unless something lower is beside it. Sundown Town leaves Informants alone. Marie Laveau's Reveal is now a hex: the strongest opposing Gate Character at her Location loses 2 Influence for the rest of the match.
- **Sundown Town and allies.** Curfew never holds Harriet Tubman herself. John Brown does not experience the town's rules: no curfew, never run out, and while he is at that Location one friendly Character who would be run out at the end of the turn (the highest Influence first) hides with him overnight instead.
- **Gate wait timing.** A Character played on turn N is Waiting through turn N+1's planning and becomes Ready at the end of N+1, so it can enter on N+2. This is the reading that makes "becomes Ready immediately" effects (Harriet, Organizer, Juneteenth) worth a full turn. If playtesting says the wait is too long, the one-line change is in the cleanup step of `resolve.ts`.
- **References.** Every history ends with a References link: a modal listing the sources for that card, Location or Threat (`src/engine/content/references.ts`), with a shareable deep link (`#refs=<id>`) into the Compendium, where each Location and Threat plate carries the same link. `npm run refs:check` confirms every URL answers; the first pass was written without network access and should be run before a release.
- **Card frames, ranked and finished.** Seventeen frames on one geometry. The ladder is bought with Legacy: Wood, Bronze (3), Silver (8), Gold (12), Emerald (16), Ruby (22), Diamond (30, never a starting rank); a card's cost sets where it starts, and the frame never outranks the card. Ten finishes (Tiger's Eye, Turquoise, Amethyst, Onyx, Marble, Ice, Camouflage, Lava, Stars and Stripes, Nature Boy) are cosmetic, for the store later; a few cards wear them and every preset deck carries one. Events keep their own frame. The Compendium's Chapter I, "Reading a card", lays out the orbs, the ladder and the finishes. `docs/economy.md` has the rules, `src/ui/legacy.ts` the ledger.
- **Stand on Business** adds a 9th turn and removes Sit Down for the player who stood. The raise is part of the hidden plan and takes effect at the end of the *following* turn. Nobody is forced into a yes/no modal: the other player plans a normal turn with a banner showing the pending raise, and Sitting Down during that turn costs only the old Legacy. The multiplier rewards and punishes the early call (×4, ×3, ×2 by the turn it is declared; standing back multiplies again, max 16), so a turn-1 Stand is a real gamble and Harborlight only makes one from an overwhelming lead. A Stand on turn 8 adds turn 9 and lands with the final result. The button itself takes its cues from Marvel Snap: stomp, stomp, clap, and on the clap a gold burst, a jolt, the Legacy coin flipping to the new price and a roar that carries on.
- **Day and night**: odd turns are day, even turns are night (`isNight`). A curfew is a condition of a Location, not a Threat: Sundown Town (`curfew: true`) lets nobody relocate out at night. The Justice System's two-turn hold is a separate rule. `lockReason(state, c)` is the one gate for both. Only curfew Locations show the time of day: a DAY or NIGHT tag on the panel, and at night `locations/<id>_night.jpg` when it exists, otherwise the day art darkened. The rest of the board looks the same day or night.
- **Harriet Tubman** (Conductor) is the only card that ignores locks: her Reveal moves any friendly Character, Gate or Inside, straight Inside another Location when there is room, otherwise to its Gates Ready. Free and before entries.
- **Relocations resolve before Reveals.** Whoever leaves a Location is gone before anything played there resolves, so a Character who has left cannot be banished from the Location they left; they stand at the Gates they arrive at when things resolve there. **Relocation** works from both zones. Inside → Gates arrives Waiting (the committed zone pays to leave). Gates → Gates keeps Ready status (the mobile zone): a Ready Character held at the Gates can be committed late wherever it is needed. One Relocation per turn, plus Porters, Lagos and Herndon exceptions.
- **Confrontation** is a planned action: commit Characters at a Threat's Location; they cannot enter or relocate that turn. Shared Threats sum both players' Force in one turn; split Threats need the owner first, then Assist is offered.
- **Word spreads.** Clearing a Threat pays +1 lasting Influence at every other open Location, hidden ones included (the wave reaches the whole board): alone you take all of it; together, three points are on the table: the larger Force share takes +1 at every other Location and the smaller takes +1 at the one other Location where it trails by the most, so a helper is never left with nothing (equal Force: both take +1 everywhere). Everyone who helped gains a Legend; at `LEGEND_READY` (2) that player's Characters arrive at the Gates Ready, played or relocated ("I have people in Detroit"). On the board nothing pours out of the Threat: a Location is ailing while a Threat sits on it (its picture drained, a red haze breathing over it), it heals the beat its last Threat breaks (the picture floods back, light sweeps up the panel), and the healing goes out as one wave of light; each Location the wave reaches blooms, sparkles twinkle over its name and its +1 comes up huge and jumps into that side's Influence circle, which flashes as it lands (every +N on the board makes the same jump; the Turn 1 blind guess reads "+1 correct guess"). The HUD carries each player's Legend.
- **"History moves"**: at the start of Turn 3 one random Threat (Patrol, Complicity, Housing Restriction or the DeWolf Trade) appears at a revealed Location without one, on Turn 5 a second one arrives in about 60% of matches, and on Turn 7 a third in about 75%. Greenwood spawns a Mob on Turn 4. Harpers Ferry reveals with Paddy Rollers.
- **Reconstruction**: a Location Lost to a Threat is not gone for good. Two turns after it falls (`RECONSTRUCTION_TURNS`), at the start of the turn, the people who stayed rebuild it: it is back in play, its Threats have moved on, and every Character still there gains +1 Influence. While a Location is Lost its Threats lie dormant (the Mob has done its work), so staying costs nothing more than the two dead turns. Greenwood's Mob arrives on Turn 4 and closes the district at the end of Turn 6 if nobody answers it (three turns unanswered); the rebuilt Greenwood returns on Turn 8, as Greenwood itself did after 1921.
- **Anansi retells**: played where the opponent leads, Anansi's Reveal draws a card and retells the Location into a random one not in this match (weighted like the setup draw, from the match RNG, so both sides of a PvP match see the same story) and spins his web over it: at a **webbed** Location, Characters costing 1 or less gain +2 Influence and Characters costing 3 or more lose 1, for both players. Characters, Influence and Threats stay; a Mob still on its way to a retold Greenwood still comes. Played where you already lead, he just draws and tricks their best Ready Gate Character into waiting again. Which Location he gets is the gamble.
- **Setback**: a loss suffered at the hands of injustice, meaning a Threat or a hostile Location working against you. The opponent's cards never cause one. **Reparations** converts Setbacks into lasting Influence on a Location: it counts at the end no matter when it is played.
- **The Justice System** Location holds anyone who goes Inside for two turns: no relocating out.
- **The Tabernacle** protects your Characters from displacement. Establish the church set there (Richard Allen, Absalom Jones, Daniel Payne) and **Black Jesus** appears: sanctuary at his Location and +1 Influence to every Character you control. He is never in a deck.
- **The Ancestors** are never in a deck either: in 25% of matches (rolled at setup), holding three Characters Inside at Accra, Ghana brings the card to your hand once. Chairteenth has the same 25% roll and arrives when Montgomery, Alabama reveals. Played while planning, it lays Harborlight's plan for the turn on the board as faint ghosts (dashed, washed out, breathing slowly: a card at the Gates it will be played to, a Character Inside where it will enter, a copy at the Gates it will move to, an Informant at your own Gates, its Event face-up at its slot, a gold ring on a Threat it will confront; `foreseePlan` in `src/ui/preview.ts`, rendered beside the real tiles so nothing is counted or moved) and says the timed dangers in a line. The AI plans deterministically, so peeking changes nothing. There is no sheet.
- **Straight Inside** (Sleeping Car Porters) always goes Inside the turn it is played. **Direct Entry** (Bessie Coleman) may: the planned move carries a Gates/Inside toggle. Nobody else skips the Gates, and nobody skips the door: a Segregationist Patrol or the Color Line turns a Straight Inside or Direct Entry arrival around too (it waits at the Gates; the Patrol charges its Setback).
- **The Mob** closes a Location after three unanswered turns.
- **Energy** shows as a Hearthstone-style crystal bar under the timer: lit crystals are unspent this turn, dim ones spent, hollow ones arrive on later turns.
- `npm run docs` regenerates `docs/copy.md` (every player-facing string, for copy editing), `docs/art-spec.md` (image specs plus the missing-art list) and `docs/art-needed.md` (the running art order, in priority order, ticked off as files land).
- **Events** are played into the purple Event slot beside your Gates at a Location: one per Location per player per turn, and the Gates can be full. Each works everywhere and gets a bonus from where it lands: Reparations +1 in the Americas, The Ancestors +1 Influence in Africa, Word of Mouth draws 2 with a crowd, Community Defense +2 Force at its Location. Decks carry at most two.
- **Gatherings** are Characters that are never in a deck. **Chairteenth** (1/3, +1 Force to friendly confronters) arrives Ready at both players' Gates the moment Juneteenth is revealed. Each arrival opens a fanfare card. If a card ever returns a Gathering to hand, it can be replayed like any Character.
- **Location scoring with two ties or a Lost Location**: whoever won more Locations wins; if equal, the brief's tiebreaks apply.
- **Katherine Johnson's** private reveal is stored per player and redacted from the opponent's view; the AI is allowed to use it exactly as a human would.
- **Victor Hugo Green** (the Green Book) lets relocations out of his Location arrive Inside, the interior-to-interior answer to Sundown Town. **Sojourner Truth** replaces the "selected additional historical figures" slot to give the pool a Suppress effect. **Ida B. Wells, Queen Nzinga, Toussaint Louverture, Marcus Garvey, Bessie Coleman** round out the sixteen.
- **Sundown Town** is drawn about a third as often as other Locations and does nothing on the turn it reveals. **Gary, Indiana** (Steel and Soul) joins the pool. **Paddy Roller** is one shared Threat "in the area" rather than one per player.
- Player Gates are shown under each Location panel (the wireframe omitted them; the brief requires them).

## Accessibility

See `docs/accessibility.md`: the card sheet is card and History only; the no-drag placement buttons stay in the code behind an assist flag (`?assist=1`), for a Settings toggle later.

## Design intents to keep

Things that play well and should survive any rebalancing:

- **The board fits the height it has.** The middle row of the app grid is a size container, and the gate tiles, Inside slots and art band are sized in `cqh` against it (with `vw`/`vh` caps), so with the browser's bars showing or in a short window they shrink together and the Inside rows and Threats are never cut off; three or more Threats at one Location stand two abreast; under 820px of height the HUD, the influence meter and the hand tighten and the "in effect" line hides. The hand sits close under the board rather than holding a band of air above itself.
- **A beat opens on the board its choreography needs, from its first frame.** The replay stages at render time (`staged` in `MatchScreen.tsx`): a clash beat opens on the board as it stood, so a knocked piece is never seen at its new place before the knock; a showdown beat keeps the Threat looking alive; an arrival's tile stays hidden until its card has flown. A Character whose entry a beat blocks stops previewing as Inside from that beat (`remainingPlan`), so the OG's block is seen to land at the Gates. Blocks, suppressions and tricks are told as a stand-off: the striker steps up once, no lunge and no impact, and the gate stays shut on each victim in turn; one man does not push everybody out.

- **Greenwood is hard on purpose.** The Mob arrives on Turn 4, closes the district after three unanswered turns, and needs 6 Force in one turn, more than either side usually has alone, so both players have to fight it together or lose the Location. When Greenwood is the last Location to reveal, players who know it start stocking up in anticipation, which is exactly the feeling we want. Do not soften the Mob, its timing, or the 6. Reconstruction does not soften it either: the district is gone for two full turns and comes back only for the last one, and Anansi retelling Greenwood does not cancel the Mob.

## PvP and the Unity port

The rules live in `src/engine` and nothing else, and that is the part that carries over. What the engine already guarantees, and what every change has to keep:

- **Pure and deterministic.** `resolveTurn(state, plans)` is a function of its inputs. All randomness comes from the match RNG seeded at `createMatch` (Location draw, Threat waves, Anansi's retell, displacement targets); the AI seeds its own RNG from the seed and turn. There is no `Math.random`, no wall clock, in the engine. Two clients with the same seed and the same two plans compute the same next state, so PvP is "exchange plans, resolve locally, compare a hash", or resolve once on a server and ship the events.
- **State is plain data.** `GameState` is JSON: no classes, no functions, no references. It serializes, diffs and replays. `viewFor(state, player)` is the redaction a server would apply before sending a client its view (hand hidden, unrevealed Locations hidden, deck order hidden); the AI is built on that same view, so nothing it does depends on secrets.
- **Plans are data.** A `TurnPlan` is a small JSON object (plays, enters, relocations, confronts, stand, sit). Validation (`validatePlan`) runs on both sides.
- **Events are the animation script.** `resolveTurn` returns an ordered event list and, with `trace: true`, one `TraceStep` per beat with the state after it. The UI replays those beats; the board animations (clash strikes, power trails) key off event `data` (`clash`, `trail`), never off text. A Unity front end reads the same events.
- **A clash is told on the board, not in a modal.** `src/ui/fly.ts` clones a tile into a fixed layer and flies the clone with the Web Animations API while the real tile sits hidden in its slot (Location panels clip and stack, so a real tile could never cross the board). One clash runs slow in, fast strike, slow out, the way Hearthstone stages an attack: the striker's ghost gathers itself (340 ms), charges to just short of the victim (280 ms), holds on impact while the board jolts, sparks spray and the Force readout floats up (`3 vs 2`), then the victim's ghost tumbles to its new Gates (or off the board toward the hand it goes back to) and its real tile pops in with the verdict stamped on it (BANISHED to Harpers Ferry, HOLDS, SENT BACK, TRICKED, HEXED, FOUND OUT…) while the striker drifts back to its slot; the banner carries the sentence throughout and the verdict holds a second to be read. Several knocks in one beat play one after another, each awaited. A Unity port does the same with a world-space clone of the tile prefab driven by the same `clash` event.
- **No explainer modals in a match.** Everything the Clash, Showdown, Arrival and Peek sheets used to say is told on the board or in the log (the sheet components remain in `Sheets.tsx`, unmounted). A **showdown** sends every confronting Character's ghost at the Threat together; the Force readout floats up; a neutralized Threat flashes red and breaks apart into its grey tile, one that holds flashes green and takes a HOLDS stamp; the fighters drift back. A **Gathering's arrival** flashes its card over the board for a second and a half, then the card flies to its tile (or to the hand it joins) and the tile pops in. **Omar's peek** lays the opponent's hand out in a strip for five seconds (tap to dismiss) and the opponent's profile sheet keeps it. The **replay banner, toasts and the peek strip sit in the lower-left corner above Sit Down** on wide screens, so nothing covers the board; on phones they stay at the top. The first-match guide and the coach's tips are off outside the tutorial. **The Reckoning** plays on the board too: when the last replay ends, each Location takes its winner's stamp (the loser's Locations first, the decisive one last), the verdict lands over the board, and the corner banner offers "See the result" and "Stay on the board". The Sit Down confirmation is the only sheet that still opens on its own. The board never changes size while you play: the hint row and the in-effect line keep one height whether or not chips are showing, so planning a card no longer shrinks the three Locations by ten pixels.
- **Content is tables.** Characters, Locations, Threats, Events and decks are typed data in `src/engine/content`; every effect is a tagged `{ type, ... }` object handled by a switch in `resolve.ts` / `query.ts`. That maps one-to-one onto ScriptableObjects and an effect enum in C#.

Porting order when the time comes: engine (TypeScript to C#, mechanical; the tests in `tests/engine.test.ts` are the spec, port them first), content tables, then the UI on top of the event stream. Keep `scripts/sim.ts` alive in both worlds: balance is measured, not argued.

## Audio

Music and sound live in `src/ui/audio` and never touch the engine. Two switches (music, effects) sit under the tagline on the landing page and beside the legacy coin in the match, remembered in `localStorage`. The music is one looping track for now, *Echoes of the Past* (`public/audio/echoes-of-the-past.m4a`; the single-file build inlines it on `window.__AUDIO__` like the art), faded in on the landing page and the match and out on the reading screens; it starts on the first tap or key, which is when browsers allow sound.

Sound effects are asked for by name (`sfx('card.drop')`), and the names are the contract: `SFX_EVENTS` in `src/ui/audio/sfx.ts` lists every cue and what it is for (tap, card pick, select and drop, lock, turn, a Location revealed, enter, move, clash hit and banish, threat spawn (a danger sting: a rising rumble, a muffled boom, the dark pulse, low brass) and clear, the cheer and the fireworks, a stand-off (the gate stays shut), trail, dig, stand, the Last Word, a Location lost, win, lose, draw). Buttons map themselves with `data-sfx="name"` (or `off`); any other button plays `tap`; the match plays one cue per replay beat and its own cues on strikes, trails and digs. The cues are recorded clips from Imphenzia's Universal Sound FX (licensed; 56 trimmed, levelled MP3s in `public/audio/sfx`, under two megabytes, chosen for a palette between Civilization, Hearthstone and Marvel Snap: real card deals for the hand, a bright two-note climb for the card you choose, three soft bells for a new turn, a case latch for Lock In, a metal gate for walking Inside, air whooshes for relocations, wood and stone impacts for strikes, church bells for The Last Word, a crowd's cheer with a rocket's launch, bursts and crackle under the fireworks when a Threat is cleared, the crowd's stomp-stomp-clap and a ring bell for the Stand on Business button (the stand landing in the replay, and each Location won at the Reckoning, keep the thud and drums), drums and the bell for a win, brass and piano for a loss and a lost Location). Every Location carries its own sound over the reveal cue: `place-<location id>.mp3`, a three-to-five-second mix of two to six clips (a till on a busy street for Greenwood, the ferry crossing the town is named for at Harpers Ferry (lapping river, oar strokes, creaking timber, a bell far off), a liner leaving port for the Black Star (the deep horn twice, the engines coming up, the pier, the water) and the horn coming in with drums on the quay for Accra, a train whistle for the Great Migration, fireworks and a bell for Juneteenth, wind and a bolt for Sundown Town, the sea heard from below, slow chains, one solemn bell and far thunder for the Middle Passage, crickets and a whisper for Charleston, the market and horns for Lagos, the mill for Gary, the gavel and cell bars for the Justice System, the bell and the congregation for the Tabernacle, a calm street and a distant bell for Montgomery, waves and birds for Oak Bluffs, a cabaret's chatter and a piano for the Stroll), listed in `SFX_PLACES` and played when that Location is revealed or arrived at (the Black Star becoming Accra sounds like Accra, after the new-turn bells; Anansi's retelling sounds at its beat); until a clip decodes the synth sketches the place where a sketch is written. `SFX_FILES` maps each name to its layers and round-robin variants; a Web Audio synth plays the same cue until the clips decode or for any name without one, so nothing is ever silent. A Unity port keeps the same names as an `AudioEvent` enum mapped to `AudioClip`s (FMOD or Wwise events if middleware comes in).

**Voice lines.** A card speaks when it is played (your own as it lands in the plan, the opponent's on its play beat), the music ducking under it. `VOICE_LINES` in `src/ui/audio/voice.ts` is both the table the player reads and the recording script: for each card, the line, whether it is a verbatim quote, an attributed or traditional saying, or a written power line, and the source. Clips go in `public/audio/voice/<card id>.mp3`; a card without one stays silent. Living people and the informants have no lines.

Other libraries worth knowing, all royalty-free for games and usable in both the web build and Unity: **Pro Sound Collection** by Gamemaster Audio (about 8,000 sounds, with casino and card categories), and for free starts **Kenney's** CC0 packs and the yearly **Sonniss GDC Game Audio Bundle**.

## Harborlight

Harborlight receives `viewFor(state, 'B')`: no opponent hand, no unrevealed Location identities, no draw order, no RNG. It decides confrontations heuristically, then enumerates plays × entries × relocations, simulates each with `resolveTurn` (opponent passing) and scores the resulting board (win probability from per-Location Influence gaps that steepen toward the final turn, projected Established value, Threat exposure, Gate crowding). It picks the best plan ~70% of the time, a sensible alternative ~20%, and an imperfect legal plan ~10%. Stand on Business triggers above ~65% / ~80% estimated win chance with a ~7% bluff rate near even. Tuning constants are in `DEFAULT_TUNING`.

On the 8+1 clock with Energy equal to the turn number and the build 136 presets (30 AI matches per pairing): Railroad vs Black Star 19–11, Black Star vs Uprising (then named Bois Caïman) 18–12, Uprising vs Pantheon 19–11, Railroad vs Pantheon 18–12, Railroad vs Mirror 13–17, no draws; a Mirror-vs-Mirror run of 40 puts every card's win rate when played between 41% and 60%, and win rate by cost across the pairings runs flat from 48% (cost 1) to 57% (cost 5), so no cost bracket is a trap. Pantheon is still the deck to tune. Plays per side run 1.3 to 1.5 a turn, hands hold near five cards, lead changes 5 to 6 a match, final-turn flips 0.6 to 1.0. (The first cut of these presets had Black Star at 7–23 against Railroad: it had traded three cheap artists for expensive cards, and tempo is bodies, so the artists came back and Williams and Seacole moved to Mirror and Pantheon.) Harborlight's stand thresholds are 0.84 / 0.94 (bluff 2%, never before Turn 5); in PvP the stand is a human call.

## Mythic and the Summon

Characters carry a category: historical, archetype or mythic. Mythic cards (Anansi, Shango, Oshun, Yemoja, Ogun, Mami Wata, Black Jesus) are fantasy drawn from African cosmology and diaspora spirituality, labelled as such on the card. The joint **Summon** is cooperative: both players commit at a Location with a Threat via quick chat; enough combined Force manifests Obatala (Threats cleared, the Location can never be Lost, +1 Influence to everyone there, both draw). A failed Summon followed by that Location being Lost costs both players 1 Influence at their other Locations.
